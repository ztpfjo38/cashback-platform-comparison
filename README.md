## What “top cashback” actually means

Cashback platforms work through affiliate commissions. You begin your shopping session on the cashback platform, click through to a participating retailer, and complete the order on the retailer’s website or app. If the purchase is tracked and meets the offer terms, the retailer pays a commission to the platform, and part of that commission is credited to your cashback balance.

The purchase price usually does not increase because you started through ShopBack. You still pay the retailer directly, while the cashback is added to your ShopBack account after the order is tracked and later confirmed.

That sounds straightforward, but the phrase **top cashback** can be misleading if it is treated as a permanent leaderboard. There are several different ways to judge whether a cashback platform is good:

- **Live rate:** How much cashback is available for the store today?
- **Merchant coverage:** Does the platform include the stores you actually use?
- **Offer clarity:** Are exclusions, product categories, and coupon rules easy to find?
- **Tracking reliability:** Does the cashback record correctly after you click through?
- **Payout method:** Can you withdraw the money in a form you will use?
- **Confirmation speed:** How long does the cashback remain pending?
- **Extra promotions:** Are boosted rates or shopping quests available?

A platform with a slightly lower headline rate can be more useful if it covers your regular stores, explains exclusions clearly, and gives you a practical payout method.

1. The headline cashback rate.

7. The expected tracking and confirmation time.

## What can cause cashback to disappear?

A cashback purchase can be rejected even when you clicked through the platform. The usual problem is not that the cashback concept failed; it is that the retailer’s conditions were not met or the tracking chain was interrupted.

Common causes include:

- Returning or cancelling the order
- Using an excluded product category
- Buying gift cards or store credit
- Applying a coupon code that was not approved for the offer
- Clicking another affiliate or cashback link afterward
- Switching from one device or browser to another
- Blocking cookies or tracking tools required by the retailer
- Using an offer after it expired
- Purchasing through a retailer app when the offer required a web session
- Making an order that the merchant classifies as invalid, fraudulent, or outside the promotion

Retailer-specific exclusions can be more important than the headline rate. One ShopBack merchant page, for example, lists gift cards, voucher purchases, taxes, fees, shipping, and orders using unsupported promotional codes among excluded transactions. The exact exclusions differ by store, so do not copy one merchant’s rules to another.

The browser extension can make activation easier on desktop, but it does not remove the need to read the offer terms. A visible activation notice confirms that cashback was enabled; it does not guarantee that every item in the final order qualifies.
